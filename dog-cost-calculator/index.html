How the estimate works

We start from a base annual cost for the dog's size class ($1,600–$3,600), split it across six categories — food & treats (28%), healthcare (30%), grooming (10%), training & services (14%), supplies (8%), and miscellaneous (10%) — then adjust for your region, care level, and the dog's age. The first year gets a 1.35× multiplier for adoption or purchase fees, setup, spay/neuter, and training. Once a dog passes its breed's senior age, the healthcare slice grows 50%. Lifetime cost assumes the breed's average lifespan. Full details in our methodology.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dog cost in the first year?

The first year typically costs about 35% more than a regular year because of adoption or breeder fees, setup supplies, spay/neuter surgery, vaccinations, and training. For a large breed on a Standard care plan, expect roughly $3,920 in year one.

What does it cost to own a dog per year after the first?

Annual costs depend mostly on size: roughly $1,600 (small), $2,200 (medium), $2,900 (large), or $3,600 (giant) on a Standard care plan, adjusted for your region. Healthcare is the largest category at about 30%.

How much does a dog cost over its whole lifetime?

Lifetime cost is first-year cost plus annual costs for each remaining year of the breed's expected lifespan. A Labrador Retriever, for example, comes to roughly $32,920 over an 11-year life on a Standard plan.

Do senior dogs cost more?

Yes. Once a dog reaches its breed's senior age, healthcare spending runs about 50% higher due to more frequent vet visits, bloodwork, dental care, and medications. This calculator applies the senior adjustment automatically when you enter a senior dog's age — or read our senior dog care guides to see why.
