What drives a Frenchie's vet bills

Brachycephalic airway syndrome (BOAS) is the breed's signature problem — the flat face means narrowed nostrils, an elongated soft palate, and a narrow trachea. Mild cases are managed; severe cases need airway surgery that typically runs $2,000–$5,000+. Because Frenchies overheat so easily, many owners also pay for pet insurance or set aside a heat-emergency fund.

Spinal issues come second: intervertebral disc disease (IVDD) and hemivertebrae (wedge-shaped vertebrae) can cause pain or paralysis. Imaging and surgery for a slipped disc can exceed $5,000, so keeping your Frenchie lean — no jumping off sofas — is genuinely money-saving advice.

Then come the chronic-but-manageable costs: skin-fold infections, allergies, and ear infections. None are catastrophic alone, but together they make the French Bulldog one of the most expensive medium-size breeds to keep healthy.

Frequently asked questions

Are French Bulldogs expensive to own?

Yes, for their size: about $2,970 the first year and $2,200 annually after, or roughly $24,970 over an 11-year life on a Standard care plan. Food is cheap for a 16–28 lb dog — it's the vet side (airway, spine, allergies) that pushes them above most medium breeds. Pet insurance is worth pricing out for this breed in particular.

What health problems should Frenchie owners budget for?

Brachycephalic airway syndrome (potentially $2,000–$5,000+ in surgery), spinal disc disease, allergies and skin-fold infections, and heat stroke risk. An emergency fund of $5,000 covers most single incidents — and buying from a breeder who health-tests (not one selling "rare colors") meaningfully lowers the odds.
