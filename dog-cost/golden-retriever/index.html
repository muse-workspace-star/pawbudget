How Much Does a Golden Retriever Cost?

The gentle, eager-to-please family favorite — with one of the highest cancer rates of any breed. Here's what a Golden really costs over a lifetime.

What drives a Golden's vet bills

Cancer is the breed's dark cloud: studies have found roughly 60% of Golden Retrievers develop cancer, with hemangiosarcoma and lymphoma the most common. Cancer treatment — surgery, chemotherapy — routinely runs $5,000–$15,000+, which dwarfs every other line item on this page. This is the single strongest financial argument for pet insurance in any breed we cover: insure early, before any diagnosis makes it a pre-existing condition.

Hip dysplasia and the resulting arthritis are the other big-ticket item, managed with medication, weight control, and occasionally $3,500–$7,000 surgery. Add the breed's dense double coat, and grooming costs run higher than the model average if you use a professional groomer — deshedding appointments add up.

Frequently asked questions

Are Golden Retrievers expensive to own?

About $3,920 the first year and $2,900 annually after — roughly $32,920 over an 11-year life on a Standard plan. The routine costs match other large breeds, but Goldens carry above-average catastrophic risk from cancer, so insurance or a robust emergency fund matters more here than for most breeds.

Should I get pet insurance for a Golden Retriever?

It's the breed where insurance makes the most financial sense: high cancer incidence plus hip-dysplasia risk means a meaningful chance of a five-figure vet bill over the dog's life. Enroll while the dog is young and healthy — pre-existing conditions aren't covered.
