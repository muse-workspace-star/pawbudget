Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to own a dog per year?

It depends heavily on size. Our model puts a typical year at roughly $1,600 for a small dog, $2,200 for a medium dog, $2,900 for a large dog, and $3,600 for a giant breed on a Standard care plan — before regional adjustments. The first year costs about 35% more due to adoption or purchase fees, setup, spay/neuter, and training.

What is the most expensive part of owning a dog?

Healthcare. Vet care is the largest single category in our model at 30% of annual spending, and it grows as dogs age — senior dogs' healthcare spending runs about 50% higher. Food is second at 28%.

How do I calculate my dog's age in human years?

Forget "multiply by 7." A 2019 UCSD study of Labrador Retrievers found the relationship is logarithmic: human-equivalent years ≈ 16 × ln(dog years) + 31, adjusted for size. Our dog age calculator applies that formula with size adjustments.

When is my dog considered a senior?

It depends on size: giant breeds around 6, large breeds around 7–8, medium breeds around 9, and small breeds around 10. See our senior-age guide for the full table and signs of aging.

Is pet insurance worth it?

It can be, especially for breeds prone to expensive conditions (hip dysplasia, cancer) and for owners who couldn't comfortably cover a $3,000–$7,000 emergency bill. Compare the monthly premium against your breed's risk profile and your emergency fund — our calculator shows both.
